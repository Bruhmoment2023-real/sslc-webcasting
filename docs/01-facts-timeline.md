Compiled 13 October 2026. Confidence labels:

## 1. The scoping note's three "facts from memory" - checked

| Claim in the scoping note | Verdict |
|---|---|
| The 2024 pass rate fell to about 73% from about 84% | **Correct [P].** Exam-1 2024: 859,967 appeared, 631,204 passed, 73.40%. March/April 2023: 83.89%. (`PN_2024_Exam1.pdf`, Table 1) |
| Webcasting was new that year | **Correct [P].** "For the first time, a new system of webcasting through CCTV's was introduced in all test centres. Control rooms were set up in all districts for monitoring the live feed." (`PN_2024_Exam1.pdf`, p. 3) |
| Grace marks were raised to soften the fall | **Correct [P], and bigger than assumed.** "However pass percentage has declined by 30% over last year. Hence the Government has decided to enhance the normalisation ... It has been decided to reduce the qualifying marks for attracting the grace from existing 35% to 25% and grace marks has been increased from 10% to 20% in all subjects as one time measure for all 3 SSLC exams of 2024. After the normalisation, the pass percentage is 73.40%" (same page) |

The 73.40% is therefore an *after-grace* figure. The before-grace figure is about 54% **[N]**
(Deccan Herald, 9 May 2024, reporting the board; consistent with the board's own "declined by
30%" **[P]**). The board has not published the before-grace figure in any table, and not for
any district.

2025 2,818; 2026 2,870. A fall of 555 centres (17%) in the webcasting year means many students
wrote away from their usual centre - a separate change that happened at the same time.

2023 14; 2024 34 (all three exams); 2025 7; 2026 9. These counts are tiny in every year and
are not a measure of how much copying there was.

## 4. Grace marks ("normalisation")

| Period | Rule | Label | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| From 2017 | Statutory rule: grace at result compilation only if the candidate "has secured aggregate marks in all subjects adequate for passing the examination, but has failed in maximum two subjects"; grace "shall not exceed more than five percent of the maximum marks of the question paper of each of the two concerned subjects" | [P] | Karnataka Secondary Education Examination Board (Scheme of Evaluation) Rules, 2017, rule 6, notification ED 465 DGW 2016(1) dated 25 Mar 2017 (`Grace_marks_notification_2017.pdf`) |
| 2021 | Raised from 5% to 10% "considering the difficulties faced by the students due to Covid-19 pandemic" | [N] | Deccan Herald, 11 May 2022, https://www.deccanherald.com/india/karnataka/kseeb-retains-10-grace-marks-for-sslc-2022-exams-1108454.html |
| 2022 | 10% retained; "the 10 per cent grace marks will be allotted to any three subjects. Earlier 5 per cent marks were distributed to two subjects." | [N] | same article |
| 2023 | 10% rule still in force | [P] by implication | The 2024 press note calls 10% and a 35% qualifying threshold the "existing" rule |
| 2024 (all three exams) | Qualifying threshold for grace cut from 35% to 25%; grace raised from 10% to 20% "in all subjects"; "one time measure" | [P] | `PN_2024_Exam1.pdf` p. 3 |
| 2024 | About 1.70 lakh students passed through grace; pass rate about 54% before, 73.40% after | [N] | Deccan Herald, 9 May 2024 and 10 Oct 2024 |
| 2024 | Passes in grade C (35-49%): 165,443 (23.66% of passes) against 19,301 (2.80%) in 2023 | [P] | `PN_2024_Exam1.pdf` Table 11 |
| 10 Oct 2024 | Minister: no grace marks from this year; "of the 20% grace marks awarded, 10% was introduced during Covid-19 pandemic, and other 10%, was in view of the reforms introduced. The 10% grace marks introduced during the pandemic will also be scrapped" | [N] | Deccan Herald, 10 Oct 2024, https://www.deccanherald.com/india/karnataka/no-grace-marks-for-sslc-from-this-year-says-madhu-bangarappa-3226507 |
| 2025 | Board "did not award grace marks, except the one allowed as per the examination ordinance to promote those on the borderline" | [N] | Deccan Herald, 2 May 2025, https://www.deccanherald.com/india/karnataka/karnataka-sslc-results-improve-by-8-percentage-points-to-6234-22-students-get-625625-2-3521589 |
| 2025 | Grade C passes back to 20,318 (3.88%) - consistent with the extra grace having gone | [P] | `PN_2025_Exam1.pdf` Table 11 |
| 2026 | Grace rule under the new pass criteria | **[U]** | Not checked |

Consequence: **three different grace regimes sit inside the comparison window.** 2019 and
2025 share the statutory 5%-in-two-subjects rule; 2022 and 2023 had the COVID 10%-in-three
rule; 2024 had 20% with a lower threshold. Before-grace and after-grace pass rates are not
published for any year except the single state-level "about 54" for 2024 [N].

  (`PN_2020_JuneJuly.pdf`).

  passed [P] (workbook). That it was a two-day multiple-choice exam is **[U]** here (from

## 9. The dated list of everything that changed alongside webcasting

For the 2023 -> 2024 comparison (all at the same exam):

1. Live webcasting of every centre to district control rooms [P]
2. Mobile-phone ban and "facing the wall" seating [N]
3. 555 fewer exam centres (3,305 -> 2,750) [P]
4. Three-exam system replaces main + supplementary [P]
5. Grace raised to 20% with a lower threshold - decided *after* seeing the results [P]
6. A different cohort: the 2024 candidates were in Classes 6-7 during the 2020-21 school closures [U as an effect; a fact as arithmetic]
7. Question-paper difficulty: unknown for 2024 [U]

For 2023 -> 2025: items 1-4, 6, 7 plus the removal of the COVID 10% grace (back to the 2017
statutory rule) [N], and a year of schools adapting.

For 2025 -> 2026: pass mark 33% with the 30-33 relaxation [N]/[P], internal marks [N], two
exams instead of three [P], departmental 29-point programme [N].
