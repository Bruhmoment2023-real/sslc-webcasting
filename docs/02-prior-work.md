## A. The model paper

1. Borcan, O., Lindahl, M. and Mitrut, A. (2017). "Fighting Corruption in Education: What
   Works and Who Benefits?" *American Economic Journal: Economic Policy* 9(1).
   https://doi.org/10.1257/pol.20150074 - working-paper version
   https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.2708374. *Metadata only.*
   (recalled) Romania's school-leaving exam: closed-circuit cameras in exam halls, rolled out
   to some counties in 2011 and all in 2012, alongside a threat of punishment for teachers.
   Pass rates fell sharply; the fall was larger for poorer students.
   **Why the Karnataka case is weaker as a design:** Romania had a staggered rollout (some
   counties a year earlier), which gives a comparison group. Karnataka covered every centre in
   the same exam. Check this description against the paper.
2. Borcan, O., Lindahl, M. and Mitrut, A. (2014). "The impact of an unexpected wage cut on
   corruption: Evidence from a 'Xeroxed' exam." *Journal of Public Economics* 120: 32-47.
   https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpubeco.2014.08.003. *Metadata only.* (recalled) Same exam;
   useful for how they measure inflated scores.

## B. Monitoring and cheating in school exams (international)

3. Bertoni, M., Brunello, G. and Rocco, L. (2013). "When the cat is near, the mice won't
   play: The effect of external examiners in Italian schools." *Journal of Public Economics*
   104: 65-77. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpubeco.2013.04.010. *Metadata only.* (recalled)
   Randomly assigned external monitors lower test scores, in monitored classes and in
   neighbouring ones.
4. Bertoni, M., Brunello, G., De Benedetto, M. A. and De Paola, M. (2021). "Does monitoring
   deter future cheating? The case of external examiners in Italian schools." *Economics
   Letters* 201: 109742. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.econlet.2021.109742. *Metadata only.*
5. Lucifora, C. and Tonello, M. (2015). "Cheating and social interactions. Evidence from a
   randomized experiment in a national evaluation program." *Journal of Economic Behavior &
   Organization* 115: 45-66. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jebo.2014.12.006. *Metadata only.*
6. Angrist, J., Battistin, E. and Vuri, D. (2017). "In a Small Moment: Class Size and Moral
   Hazard in the Italian Mezzogiorno." *American Economic Journal: Applied Economics* 9(4):
   216-249. https://doi.org/10.1257/app.20160267. *Metadata only.* (recalled) Apparent
   class-size effects in southern Italy are score manipulation.
7. Battistin, E., De Nadai, M. and Vuri, D. (2017). "Counting rotten apples: Student
   achievement and score manipulation in Italian elementary Schools." *Journal of
   Econometrics* 200: 344-362. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jeconom.2017.06.015. *Metadata only.*
8. Jacob, B. and Levitt, S. (2003). "Rotten Apples: An Investigation of the Prevalence and
   Predictors of Teacher Cheating." *Quarterly Journal of Economics* 118(3): 843-877.
   https://doi.org/10.1162/00335530360698441. *Metadata only.*
9. Martinelli, C., Parker, S., Perez-Gea, A. C. and Rodrigo, R. (2018). "Cheating and
   Incentives: Learning from a Policy Experiment." *American Economic Journal: Economic
   Policy* 10(1): 298-325. https://doi.org/10.1257/pol.20150066. *Metadata only.* (recalled)
   Mexico: cash incentives for scores increased copying.
10. Berkhout, E., Pradhan, M., Rahmawati, Suryadarma, D. and Swarnata, A. (2024). "Using
    technology to prevent fraud in high stakes national school examinations: Evidence from
    Indonesia." *Journal of Development Economics* 170: 103307.
    https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jdeveco.2024.103307 - working paper "From Cheating to Learning"
    https://doi.org/10.35489/bsg-rise-wp_2020/046. *Metadata only.* (recalled) Phased move to
    computer-based testing cut scores most where earlier scores looked most inflated. **This
    is the closest developing-country design to the Karnataka question and probably a better
    model than the Romanian paper**, because it is about "the drop reveals prior inflation".
11. Cagala, T., Glogowsky, U. and Rincke, J. (2021/2024). "Detecting and Preventing Cheating
    in Exams." *Journal of Human Resources* 59: 210-241.
    https://doi.org/10.3368/jhr.0620-10947r1. *Metadata only.* University setting.

## D. India

14. Kingdon, G. and Muzammil, M. (2013). "The School Governance Environment in Uttar Pradesh,
    India: Implications for Teacher Accountability and Effort." *Journal of Development
    Studies* 49(2): 251-269. https://doi.org/10.1080/00220388.2012.700397. *Metadata only.*
    (recalled, **verify before use**) Discusses the 1992 Uttar Pradesh anti-copying ordinance
    and the collapse of the board pass rate that year.
15. Singh, A. (2020). "Myths of Official Measurement: Auditing and Improving Administrative
    Data in Developing Countries." RISE Working Paper 20/042.
    https://doi.org/10.35489/bsg-rise-wp_2020/042. *Metadata only.* (recalled) Official test
    data in an Indian state were inflated; tablet-based testing reduced it.
16. **Uttar Pradesh board 2018 - news only, no study found.** CCTV in all centres for the
    first time; pass percentage 75.16 against 81.18 the year before for high school;
    about 10.4 lakh candidates (about 15%) absent in the first four days; 150 schools with no
    pass. Sources surfaced by search and **not opened**: Hindustan Times (Lucknow) 30 Apr 2018
    "CCTV cameras, STF caused dip in pass percentage",
    https://www.pressreader.com/india/hindustan-times-lucknow/20180430/281951723434749 ;
    careers360, https://news.careers360.com/zero-pass-percentage-150-board-schools-result-clampdown-cheating-or-evidence-poor-education-quality ;
    The Quint, https://www.thequint.com/news/up-board-exams-students-fail-to-appear .
    Treat every number here as "news, search snippet only".
17. **Bihar board 2016 - not researched.** The well-known fall in the Class 12 pass rate after
    the 2015 cheating photographs is from memory only **[unconfirmed]**; no source was opened.
18. **Karnataka.** Crossref returned one loosely related item: Narayana, M. R. (2000).
    "Determinants of Pass Percentage of Students in Aided Private Degree Colleges: Evidence
    for Karnataka." *Artha Vijnana* 42(1). https://doi.org/10.21648/arthavij/2000/v42/i1/115907

## Novelty verdict

- **No study of the Karnataka 2024 webcasting episode was found** in Crossref, the partial
  Semantic Scholar results, or two web searches; the web search returned only news coverage.
- **The search is incomplete** (OpenAlex unavailable; Google Scholar, SSRN, RePEc and
  Shodhganga not searched). The honest statement today is: *"We are not aware of a published
  study"*, not *"this is the first study"*.
- Before writing a novelty claim Sean should run, and record the dates of, searches on Google
  Scholar, SSRN and IDEAS for: "SSLC webcasting", "Karnataka SSLC 2024 pass percentage",
  "exam webcasting India", "CCTV board examination India", "KSEAB grace marks".
